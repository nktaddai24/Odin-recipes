Description

Ghana Jollof rice is a legendary dish that embodies the heart of West African cuisine. It begins with long-grain rice simmered in a rich tomato-based sauce made from blended tomatoes, onions, peppers, and a touch of ginger and garlic. The sauce is seasoned with spices like curry powder, bay leaves, and thyme, creating a fragrant base that infuses the rice with bold, savory flavors. Often, chicken, beef, or fish is added to enhance the dish, while the slow cooking process ensures the rice absorbs every bit of the vibrant sauce. The result is a smoky, slightly spicy, and deeply satisfying meal that is as much a cultural symbol as it is a culinary delight.

Beyond its taste, Jollof rice carries a sense of pride and tradition. In Ghana, it is a centerpiece at celebrations, weddings, and family gatherings, often served alongside fried plantains, grilled meats, or fresh salads. Its bright red-orange hue makes it visually striking, while the balance of textures—soft rice, tender meat, and crisp sides—creates a feast for both the eyes and palate. The dish is also part of the famous “Jollof wars,” a friendly rivalry among West African nations over whose version reigns supreme. Ghana’s take is cherished for its depth of flavor and smoky undertones, making it a dish that impresses not only in taste but also in cultural significance.

Ingredients

  • Long grain rice
  • Large fresh tomatoes
  • tomato paste
  • Onions
  • Cloves garlic
  • Ginger
  • Fresh chili peppers
  • Curry powder
  • Teaspoon thyme
  • Bay leaves
  • Chicken or vegetable sauce
  • Vegetable oil
  • Salt to taste
  • Protein of choice: beef, fish, or gaot

Steps

  1. Blend tomatoes, one onion, garlic, ginger, and peppers into a smooth paste. Heat oil in a large pot, add sliced onions, then stir in tomato paste and cook until it reduces and darkens. Add the blended mixture and simmer until the raw tomato flavor cooks out.
  2. Season with curry powder, thyme, bay leaves, paprika, and salt. Pour in the stock and bring to a boil. Add the washed rice, stir, and reduce heat to low. Cover tightly and allow the rice to steam, stirring occasionally to prevent burning.
  3. Once the rice is cooked and fluffy, add your pre-cooked protein and mix gently. Serve hot with fried plantains, salad, or grilled meat for a complete Ghanaian feast.
